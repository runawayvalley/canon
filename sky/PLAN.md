# Canon in D — Sky Edition: Plan

## Goal

Reimagine Pachelbel's *Canon in D* as a **Studio Ghibli–style flight scene**, in the spirit of Joe Hisaishi's scores: a nostalgic solo piano opening, then lush strings, woodwinds and warm horns that lift off into a joyful, wide-open flight over hills and clouds. The emotional arc is **wonder → joy → nostalgia**, not epic triumph. The melody can be reshaped and the key can move, but listeners must still recognize it as *Canon in D*.

## What must stay (identity anchors)

1. **The ground bass.** The 8-note, 2-bar ostinato (original: `D – A – B – F# – G – D – G – A`), transposed with the key. It is present in every cycle except the one marked bridge (see Structure).
2. **The 8-chord progression** (I – V – vi – iii – IV – I – IV – V), recolored but the same shape.
3. **The canon.** 3 upper voices play the same line, each entering **2 bars** after the previous one, at least in sections 3, 4 and 6.
4. **The famous opening line.** `F# – E – D – C# – B – A – B – C#` (scale degrees 3–2–1–7–6–5–6–7). Its contour stays intact every time it's played; only rhythm and ornaments change.
5. **Variation-over-a-ground form.** Each cycle adds something: a new voice, more motion, or more height.

## Ghibli / Hisaishi style traits to aim for

- **Piano-led and intimate first.** Solo piano, played freely (rubato), before the orchestra enters.
- **Singable, lyrical melody.** Stepwise lines with expressive leaps (rising 6ths, octaves), long held notes and appoggiaturas (a note leaning onto a chord tone), plus grace notes and dotted "skipping" rhythms in the lighter sections.
- **Pentatonic tint.** A Japanese-flavoured major scale without the 4th and 7th degrees, used in the counter-melodies and the flute/glockenspiel lines. The Canon theme itself keeps its 7th (C#), because it's part of the identity.
- **Warm, rich harmony.** maj7, add9 and sus chords; secondary dominants; IVmaj7 → V → iii7 → vi7 moves; and chromatic-mediant colour chords (e.g. ♭VI) at emotional peaks. Avoid harsh "epic trailer" power chords.
- **Classic orchestra, not synths or SFX.** Strings, flute, oboe, clarinet, horns, harp, glockenspiel, piano, light timpani. "Wind" comes from orchestral writing (string tremolo, harp glissandi, flute runs), not from a noise layer.
- **Breathing tempo.** Gentle ritardandos at phrase ends and a short pause before big entries, like a film cue following the picture.

## Key plan

| Stage | Key | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On the hill (opening) | **D major** | Starts as the familiar Canon, solo piano. |
| Take-off → flight | **E♭ major** (+1 semitone) | A soft, warm lift. E♭ suits horns and woodwinds, and gives a mellower Hisaishi-like colour than bright E. |
| Above the clouds (climax) | **F major** (+2 more) | The highest, fullest moment. Reached through a ♭VI → ♭VII → I lift (D♭ → E♭ → F). |
| Landing | **F major**, then piano alone ends in **F** on Fadd9 | Nostalgic, open ending, not a heavy cadence. |

Each modulation is **prepared** (pivot chord or a ♭VI–♭VII lift plus a short pause), never a bare pop-style truck-driver key change.
Fallback: stay in D throughout and rise only through register and orchestration, if the modulations weaken recognizability.

## Melody plan

- **Theme A (identity):** the opening line, stretched to half notes in the strings/horns over the moving texture, with appoggiaturas at bar starts.
- **Theme B (flight):** a new soaring counter-melody in the Hisaishi manner, rising 6th + stepwise fall, built over the same ground, for flute → violins → horns.
- **Ornamental voice:** the original's 8th/16th-note variations rewritten as pentatonic runs for flute, harp and glockenspiel (gusts of wind, birds).
- **Rule:** each theme must fit the canon (works against itself at a 2-bar offset). Check for clashes in code before orchestrating.

## Structure (draft, ~4:15)

Each "cycle" = one 2-bar pass of the ground bass.

| # | Section | Cycles | Key | Tempo | Content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **On the Hill** | 1–4 | D | ~58, rubato | Solo piano: ground in the left hand, Theme A in the right. Nostalgic, quiet. |
| 2 | **The Breeze** | 5–8 | D | 62 | Cello takes the ground, harp rolled chords, flute sings Theme A, violin Theme B, flute pentatonic answers. Solo melodies only (the strict canon starts at Take-off, to keep its 2-bar spacing). |
| 3 | **Take-off** | 9–12 | D → E♭ | 62 → 70 | Voices 2 and 3 enter (+2, +4 bars). String tremolo + harp glissando swell, short breath, then the lift into E♭. |
| 4 | **Flight** | 13–20 | E♭ | 72–76 | Full canon with new variations (8th-note run, soaring half notes, skipping dotted rhythm, rising answer; at most one voice in 8ths). Horns sing Theme B and Theme A, glockenspiel sparkle, light timpani pulse. |
| 5 | **Through the Clouds (bridge)** | 21–24 | E♭ (♭VI colour) | 72, feel slows | The only cycles allowed to leave the ground: a Hisaishi-style IVmaj7 – V – iii7 – vi7 interlude. Thin texture: high strings, celesta, solo oboe. Ends with a ♭VI–♭VII lift. |
| 6 | **Above the Clouds (climax)** | 25–32 | F | 76 | Full orchestra; Theme A in horns + violins in octaves, Theme B against it, canon at full height. |
| 7 | **Landing** | 33–36 | F | 76 → 50 rit. | Voices fall away one by one. Solo piano returns with Theme A, slowing, ends on Fadd9. |

Approximate timing: cycles 1–12 ≈ 1:35, cycles 13–32 ≈ 2:10, landing ≈ 0:30 → about 4:15 plus rubato and tails. The tempo stays relaxed throughout (max ~76 BPM, with one chord per beat); the sense of flight comes from faster note values (8ths/16ths), not a faster beat.

## Tooling

Reuse `nightmare/src/lib.py` (Score, tempo map, MIDI writer) and `render_sf2.py` (GeneralUser GS render), imported from `sky/src/`. Gaps to fill:

- **Expression / swells.** `lib.py` only has per-note velocity. Add CC11/CC7 expression curves (crescendos, swells into lifts) or emulate them with velocity ramps plus stem gain automation.
- **Rubato.** Use dense tempo-map points for the piano sections and ritardandos.
- **Humanization.** Small timing (±10 ms) and velocity jitter, so the strings and piano don't sound mechanical.
- **Sustain pedal (CC64)** for the piano.

## Review checks

- **Recognizable:** Theme A is clearly audible within the first ~15 s, and the ground bass is audible in every cycle except the bridge.
- **Ghibli feel:** piano opening with rubato; warm maj7/add9 harmony; woodwinds and horns carry melodies; no synth pads, trailer hits or noise effects.
- **Dynamic arc:** quiet opening → gradual lift → loudest at the climax → quiet piano ending (check with RMS per 15 s window, as in nightmare milestone 5).
- **Clean canon:** no unintended semitone clashes between the voices on strong beats.

## Open questions

- **Meter:** 4/4 throughout (default), or switch the Flight section to a 3/4 waltz lilt (Hisaishi often uses waltzes)? A 3/4 version would stretch the ground to 3 bars and weaken the canon's 2-bar spacing.
- **Key changes:** D → E♭ → F (default), or stay in D?
- **Length:** ~4:15, or trim the Flight section (cycles 13–20) to get under 4 minutes?
